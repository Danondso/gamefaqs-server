--- answer ---
To beat Sephiroth in Final Fantasy VII, you have two main options:

1. Allow Sephiroth to attack you, reducing your HP to critical. His AI script will then take control of Cloud and force him to use "Attack" on Sephiroth, triggering a counter script that ends the battle [7]. This method creates an Auto-Counter effect without needing the Counter Attack Materia [7].

2. Use the Omnislash command as intended, which will knock Sephiroth down and end the battle [7]. 

It is also worth noting that if you use hacks to enable Materia and Commands, you can end the battle by using Steal on Sephiroth, as this triggers the counter-script [7]. Additionally, if Sephiroth's attack misses, Cloud will still counter and end the battle [7]. 

The final encounter is a battle of the mind, where Sephiroth is a manifestation of his own will, and by this point he is too weak to withstand Cloud's strength, which is why he only has 1 HP [7]. The "Those Chosen By The Planet (Intro)" Theme plays throughout the battle, and Cloud does not perform his victory pose [7].

--- answer ---
To beat the Elite Four in Pokémon Red Version, follow the strategies and team recommendations from the provided guides:

**Elite Four: Lorelei**  
- Location: Indigo Plateau  
- Pokémon: Dewgong L54, Cloyster L53, Slowbro L54, Jynx L56, Lapras L56  
- Strategy: Use Electric-type Pokémon (e.g., Raichu, Jolteon, Electabuzz, Zapdos) to defeat her Ice/Water-type Pokémon. Thunderbolt is effective against Dewgong, Cloyster, and Slowbro; Thunder is effective against Jynx and Lapras. Alternatively, use Fire-type Pokémon (e.g., Charizard, Flareon, Magmar) to counter Ice and Water types, but be prepared to handle Slowbro with a different type. Avoid Grass-type Pokémon for Jynx, as it is weak to them. Keep HP high with Max Potions and watch out for Aurora Beam (from Dewgong and Cloyster) and Hydro Pump (from Lapras) [1, 2, 4, 7].

**Elite Four: Bruno**  
- Location: Indigo Plateau  
- Pokémon: Onix L53, Hitmonchan L55, Hitmonlee L55, Onix L56, Machamp L58  
- Strategy: Use Psychic-type Pokémon (e.g., Alakazam) to easily defeat Hitmonchan, Hitmonlee, and Machamp. Ground, Water, or Grass-type Pokémon can defeat the Onix. Zapdos’s Drill Peck is also effective against Bruno’s team [1, 2, 4, 7].

**Elite Four: Agatha**  
- Location: Indigo Plateau  
- Pokémon: Gengar L56, Golbat L56, Haunter L55, Arbok L58, Gengar L60  
- Strategy: Use Psychic-type Pokémon (e.g., Alakazam) or Electric-type Pokémon (e.g., Zapdos) to counter Ghost-types, as they are immune to physical attacks. Agatha’s Pokémon are weak to these types, and her fighting style is not strong [7].

**Elite Four: Lance**  
- Location: Indigo Plateau  
- Pokémon: Gyarados L58, Dragonair L56, Dragonair L56, Aerodactyl L60, Dragonite L62  
- Strategy: Use Ice-type Pokémon (e.g., Articuno, Jolteon, or a strong Ice Beam/Blizzard move) to counter Dragon-types, as they are weak to Ice attacks. Gyarados and Aerodactyl can be defeated with minimal effort [7].

**General Tips**:  
- Assemble a well-balanced team that can counter Ice, Fighting, Ghost, and Dragon types [4].  
- You cannot return to heal between battles; only use healing items during the fight [2].  
- Bring Max Potions and other healing items before entering the Elite Four challenge [4].  
- After defeating the Elite Four, defeat Gary, the Pokémon Champion, to complete the game [1].  
- After the credits, you can replay the game with your data to train Level 100 Pokémon [1].

--- answer ---
To beat Krauser in Resident Evil 4, follow these steps:

1. After the conversation with Krauser, quickly eliminate the incoming Seeker robots [6].
2. Pick up the Yellow Herb and enter the doorway with the spiral stairs [6].
3. Climb the ladder to reach the top of Tower 2 [6].
4. Turn left and run to the far end to collect a Green Herb and Magnum Ammo (3) [6].
5. Take the second piece, the Piece of the Holy Beast, Eagle [6].
6. Press L+R to dodge Krauser’s first attack, which initiates the fight and starts a 3-minute countdown [6].
7. To start the fight, run all the way past Krauser and reenter the Laboratory room. This causes Krauser to be on the opposite side when you return, allowing you to get two clear shots at him with a Rifle [7].
8. To damage Krauser, first target his legs using TMP ammo or sniping with a Rifle. Once he is down, his upper body will be exposed, allowing you to deal significant damage [7].
9. Use the HandCannon to stop Krauser during the fight, as it shortens the encounter [8].
10. Dodge Krauser’s attacks using L+R or A+B, as he relies on attacks that can be dodged this way [7].
11. When Krauser throws a Flash Grenade, it means he has had enough [7].
12. After defeating Krauser, grab the last insignia piece and use it at the wall to progress [8].

This strategy is detailed in the section "Walkthrough — Chapter 5-3: Krauser (Cut Scene Fight)" [4].

Q [specific]: How do I get all 120 stars in Super Mario 64?
no_answer: false
recall hit: true → "Super Mario 64"
keyword hits: 1/1 ["stars"]
--- answer ---
To get all 120 stars in Super Mario 64, follow the steps and strategies outlined in the guide:

1. Complete the game’s main objectives by collecting all 120 Power Stars across various worlds and levels, including the Castle Secret Stars and other course-specific stars [1, 4, 5, 8].

2. For specific stars, use the following methods:
   - **Princess Daisy's Secret Slide**: Complete it under 20 seconds (not 15, as that is impossible) to unlock Luigi [2].
   - **Jolly Roger Bay – Sunken Ship**: Drain the ship, then run around each chest 64 times clockwise and 64 times counterclockwise, then butt-stomp the chest closest to you to obtain a hat with an "L" [2].
   - **Can the Eel Come out to Play? (Star #17)**: After obtaining the hat with an "L", go to the eel star, which is blue and hard to see; grab the eel’s tail to complete the star [2].
   - **Bowser in the Dark World (Star #28)**: Defeat Bowser using the strategy of circling him and grabbing his tail; after three hits, Bowser is defeated [5].
   - **Tick Tock Clock (Star #2)**: Press the star switch and collect the switch star before time runs out [7].
   - **Rainbow Ride (Star #7)**: Collect five silver stars to unlock the power star [7].
   - **Goomboss Battle (Star #5)**: Collect all 8 red coins in the level to earn the power star [8].
   - **Castle Courtyard 8 Red Coins (Star #7)**: Defeat eight ghosts in the courtyard to earn their eight red coins [8].

3. After collecting all 120 stars, return to the castle courtyard. On the right side of the starting area, a cannon hole will open. Use the cannon to shoot up onto the roof [5].

4. On the roof, Yoshi will appear and give you 100 extra lives [5]. You will also find a Wing Cap [5].

Q [specific]: How do I beat Lavos in Chrono Trigger?
no_answer: false

--- answer ---
To beat Lavos in Chrono Trigger, follow these steps:

1. Ensure you are at the End of Time. Talk to the old man there to learn how and when you can fight Lavos [7].
2. Assemble your party and complete any side quests you wish before fighting Lavos [7].
3. Rest and save your game [7].
4. Go to the bucket in the upper-right corner of the End of Time and use it to warp to 1999 A.D. [7].
5. When you arrive in 1999 A.D., head north to witness Lavos emerging from the ground [7].
6. Choose to fight Lavos when prompted [7].
7. The battle against Lavos is a multi-part battle involving three parts: the Center Bit, the Lavos Core, and the Left Bit [1].
   - The Center Bit has 10,000 HP, Attack 100, and Magic 21 [1].
   - The Lavos Core has 30,000 HP and Defense 255 [1].
   - The Left Bit has 2,000 HP and absorbs Fire, Lightning, Shadow, and Water attacks [1].
   - All parts have Speed 16, Evade 20, and Hit 100 [1].
8. The battle is extremely difficult and requires high-level characters and strong equipment [3].
   - Recommended requirements: Level 60+, Power, Magic, Magic Def, and Speed all at Maximum [3].
   - Required equipment: Crono’s Rainbow, Gold Stud, Safe Helm, and Nova Armor [3].
9. Only Crono can fight Lavos in this battle; it is a one-on-one fight [3].
10. After defeating Lavos, the game concludes with a special ending where Crono is teleported to the development room, where you meet characters like Gaspar, Spekkio, and a Nu [3].

Note: This battle can be triggered earlier by examining the right pod of Lucca’s teleportation device in Leene’s Square with Marle in your party, but this leads to a very difficult fight and is only possible in New Game+ mode [6].

    improved, leeching FA, but at the same time gave us Cold Immune monsters. No
    fun. At the same time, the role of FA changed completely: because of a bug, FA

    level FA makes it worth maxing this skill.

    XIII.  SHRINES
    
    Scattered randomly across the vast lands of Diablo II are various types of
    shrines.  Each on has a different temporary effect on you.  Most of them are
    good, but some can have a negative effect.  Here is a description of all the
    shrines:
    
    -Refilling Shrine-
    Recovers your health and mana to max.  Try to use these only when you need
    them.  Don't touch them if you need to get back those 5 health you lost.
    
    -Health Shrine-
    Recovers your health to max.  Once again, try and save these for when you
    need them.
    
    -Mana Shrine-
    Recovers your mana to max.  These are only really useful for spellcasters.
    
    -Armor Shrine-
    Doubles your defense.  This means attacks are less likely to hit you and
    will do less damage.  These are most useful when you are surrounded by
    enemies.
    
    -Combat Shrine-
    Increases you damage and attack rating by 200%.  This will really let you
    lay down some damage.  These rule if they are near a boss.
    
    -Resist Shrines-
    Come in Resist Fire, Resist Poison, Resist Lightning, and Resist Cold.  They
    increase your resistance to a specific element.  They can be useful but
    usually you won't need them.
    
    -Mana Recharge Shrine-
    Increases the rate at which your Mana refills by 400%.  This is very cool
    for spell casters.
    
    -Skill Shrine-
    This shrine adds +2 to any skills you have already learned.  This is
    a pretty sweet shrine.  Good for any time use.
    
    -Stamina Shrine-

Q [specific]: What's the missingno glitch in Pokemon Red?
no_answer: false
recall hit: true → "Pokemon Red Version — Xabbott)"
keyword hits: 2/3 ["Cinnabar","old man","item"]
--- answer ---
The MissingNo glitch in Pokémon Red Version is a game exploit that allows players to encounter a glitch Pokémon named MissingNo., which is not a real Pokémon but a programming error. It can be triggered in several ways:

1. **Cinnabar Island Surfing Method**: Trade a Venonat for a Tangela or another Pokémon at the Pokémon Lab, then surf along the coast of Cinnabar Island, specifically on half-land-half-water tiles near the Pokémon Gym. If done correctly, a battle with MissingNo. will occur [5].

2. **Safari Zone Glitch**: Surfing along the coast after visiting the Safari Zone can cause Pokémon from the Safari Zone to appear, but this method may also trigger the MissingNo. glitch [5].

3. **Letter-Based Glitch**: Using specific letters in the Pokémon name (particularly letters 3, 5, and 7 of the name) can cause the game to access glitch data, potentially resulting in encountering MissingNo. [6]. Letters such as G, H, J, M, S, V, W, X, Y, and Z can lead to MissingNo. depending on the combination [6].

MissingNo. is a glitch Pokémon with a level of 80, a Normal/Flying type, and has two Water Gun moves (PP 20/20) and one Sky Attack (PP 5/5). It has extremely high attack (around 150) but extremely low defense (about 20) at level 80 [1]. 

The glitch can cause serious consequences, including save file corruption, permanent damage to the cartridge, graphical glitches, freezing, and corruption of the Hall of Fame data [3]. It may also cause the game to hang or fail to end battles properly [3].

One of the benefits of encountering MissingNo. is that it can increase the quantity of the sixth item in the player’s inventory by 128, allowing for nearly unlimited TMs, Rare Candies, and stat-boosting items [8]. This is due to the glitch exploiting a flag that controls item counts [8].

Additionally, the glitch can be used to obtain other Pokémon through the "Ditto Glitch" or "Fight-cancelled Battle Manipulation" (also known as the Oddish Glitch), where the Special stat of the last Pokémon fought determines the Pokémon accessed [4]. This can allow players to catch Pokémon like Mew or Oddish [4].

Despite rumors, MissingNo. is not the Pokémon Ash Ketchum saw in the first episode of the Pokémon anime (which is Hohou, a Pokémon numbered 250), nor is it a real Pokémon [1]. It is a glitch, and its existence is tied to the game’s programming errors [1].
